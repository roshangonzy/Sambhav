Section C (10 Marks)

  • In solid state, ions are locked in a rigid crystal lattice by strong electrostatic forces (no mobile ions). In aqueous solution, the lattice breaks, releasing free, mobile ions to carry charge.
    (1 mark for "no free ions in solid", 1 mark for "free mobile ions in aqueous").
  • A large amount of thermal energy is required to break the strong electrostatic forces of attraction between oppositely charged ions in the lattice.
    (1 mark for "strong electrostatic forces", 1 mark for "large energy required to break them").
  • Dry HCl is a polar covalent molecule (no free ions). Water is a polar solvent that causes HCl to ionize into $H_3O^+$ and $Cl^-$ ions, enabling conductivity.
    (1 mark for "no ions in dry gas", 1 mark for "water causes ionization").
  • $CCl_4$ has a perfectly symmetrical tetrahedral shape. The individual bond polarities pull equally in opposite directions and cancel each other out entirely (net dipole moment is zero).
    (1 mark for "symmetrical structure", 1 mark for "polarities cancel out").
  • They already possess a stable octet (or duplet for He) in their outermost valence shell. They do not need to lose, gain, or share electrons.
    (1 mark for "stable electronic configuration / octet", 1 mark for "no need to transfer/share").
